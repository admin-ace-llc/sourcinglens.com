CAPE Declaration: Step-by-Step Guide to Filing Your IEEPA Tariff Refund

📅 June 2026 ⏱ 8 min read ✍️ SourcingLens

Who this is for: US importers who paid IEEPA tariffs between Feb 4, 2025 and Feb 24, 2026 and want to file their own CAPE Declaration as Importer of Record — without needing a customs broker.

CBP opened the CAPE (Consolidated Administration and Processing of Entries) portal on April 20, 2026. It's the only official channel for claiming IEEPA tariff refunds. This guide walks through the complete process — from getting ACE access to receiving your ACH deposit.

Before you start: your pre-flight checklist

  • You are listed as the Importer of Record on your Form 7501 entry summaries
  • Your entries include dates between Feb 4, 2025 and Feb 24, 2026
  • You (or your broker) filed the entries directly — entries filed by a broker require the broker to submit unless they've authorized you
  • You have or can get an ACE Secure Data Portal account
  • You have your bank routing number and account number ready for ACH enrollment

Step 1 — Get ACE Secure Data Portal access

1

Apply for or verify your ACE account

Go to ace.cbp.dhs.gov and log in. If you don't have an account, apply through CBP's portal at cbp.gov. Account provisioning typically takes 2–3 business days. You need an Importer account (not an Exporter account).

Already using a customs broker? If your broker filed your entries on your behalf, they likely already have ACE access for your account. Contact them — they can submit the CAPE Declaration directly, which is often faster than setting up your own IOR access.

Step 2 — Enroll in ACH for refund payments

2

Add your bank details to ACE

In your ACE portal, navigate to your Importer sub-account. Find the revenue/payment section and add your ACH bank routing number and account number. This step is mandatory — CBP cannot issue refunds without it, and this detail is not shared with your broker's account automatically.

Step 3 — Pull your entry history

3

Export your import entries from ACE

In ACE, run an entry history report for the period February 4, 2025 through February 24, 2026. Export it as a CSV. The report should include entry numbers, entry dates, liquidation dates, HTS classifications, and duty amounts. Filter for entries where Chapter 99 codes (9903.01.xx or 9903.08.xx) appear — these are your IEEPA-eligible entries.

If you can't access ACE entry history directly, your customs broker can pull this report. Alternatively, gather your Form 7501 entry summaries — every shipment has one.

Step 4 — Build your CAPE CSV

4

Format your entry list exactly as CBP requires

The CAPE Declaration CSV is simple — a single column of entry numbers, one per row. Maximum 9,999 entries per declaration (submit multiple files if you have more).

Entry Number
SFO-2847391-0
LAX-3910284-2
JFK-5501938-4
CHI-1029384-7
... (up to 9,999 rows per file)

Only include Phase 1 eligible entries in your first CAPE Declaration. These are entries that are currently unliquidated, or that liquidated within the past 80 days. Including already-liquidated entries older than 80 days won't cause errors but those entries will need to wait for Phase 2 processing.

Step 5 — Submit via the CAPE tab in ACE

5

Upload your CSV to the CAPE module

Log into ACE, navigate to the CAPE tab (added April 20, 2026). Click "New CAPE Declaration," upload your CSV file, and submit. CBP validates the entry numbers against their database. You'll receive a confirmation number.

Step 6 — Wait for ACH deposit

6

Track your refund status in ACE

CBP processes accepted declarations within 60–90 days. Once entries are liquidated or reliquidated, ACH deposits arrive within 3–5 weeks. Your refund includes the original IEEPA duty amount plus 6% annual statutory interest calculated from your original payment date to the refund date.

What about Phase 2?

Phase 2 will cover entries that were liquidated more than 80 days before your CAPE filing date. CBP has confirmed Phase 2 is coming but has not announced a launch date. These entries are still refundable — they just can't be included in Phase 1 CAPE Declarations.

Recommendation: file Phase 1 now to start the clock on your most time-sensitive entries, then file Phase 2 when CBP opens that window. Our tool flags which of your entries fall into each phase.

Can I file if my entries were filed by a broker?

Yes — there are two paths:

  1. Ask your broker to file CAPE on your behalf. They're already your authorized agent and have your entry history. Many brokers are handling this as a routine service.
  2. File yourself as IOR. Even if your broker filed the original entries, you as the Importer of Record can submit a CAPE Declaration for those same entries via your own ACE account.
