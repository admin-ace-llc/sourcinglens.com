Common questions

What is an IEEPA tariff refund?

In February 2026, the US Supreme Court ruled that tariffs imposed under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act (IEEPA) — including the "reciprocal" tariffs from April 2025 — were unlawful. CBP must refund all IEEPA duties paid between February 4, 2025 and February 24, 2026, plus statutory interest (~7% annually).

What documents do I need to upload?

The easiest source is your ACE Secure Data Portal entry history export (a CSV containing all your entry summaries). Alternatively, upload your CBP Form 7501 PDFs — one per shipment, up to 20 at a time. Your customs broker can also provide either of these.

What is the CAPE CSV and how do I use it?

CAPE (Consolidated Administration and Processing of Entries) is CBP's portal for submitting IEEPA refund claims. You upload a CSV of your eligible entry numbers into the CAPE tab in ACE. Our tool generates this CSV pre-formatted to CBP specifications. You or your licensed customs broker uploads it to ACE to initiate your refund.

What's the difference between Phase 1 and Phase 2?

Phase 1 covers entries that are unliquidated or liquidated within 80 days of your CAPE submission date. Phase 2 covers older liquidated entries and is expected to open later. We flag which of your entries fall into each phase so you know what to prioritize.

Do you file the CAPE Declaration on my behalf?

No — only the Importer of Record (that's you) or your licensed customs broker can submit the CAPE Declaration to CBP. We prepare all the documents; you or your broker submits them. This keeps us in the document preparation lane and you in full control of your filing.

Are my documents stored or shared?

No. Your documents are processed in your browser session and via our secure serverless function — never stored on our servers. The analysis results exist only in your browser session until you download them.
